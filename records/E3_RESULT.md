**Verdict — Outcome C of §6.** The adaptive arm does not beat the frozen comparator on either axis.
It is beaten on net (+1,194 vs +1,583), on Sharpe (1.53 vs 2.35), on drawdown (188.9 vs 103.6), on
break-even cost (0.483 vs 1.110 per leg), and on the mechanism diagnostic itself (E[ω/δ] 0.936 vs
0.837).

Section 7 of the manuscript stands. E2 established that the range regime is identifiable at 10:30;
that identification does not transfer into profitable threshold control. The information constraint
is confirmed, and now described more precisely than "adaptive thresholds fail".

The adaptive arm is not worthless: it is net positive in every one of the six calendar years
(+19.9, +267.7, +317.9, +97.8, +278.8, +211.6) and beats four of the six fixed thresholds. It simply
loses to the two that a constant choice would have found.

## Secondary result, not pre-registered as a hypothesis

Across the six fixed arms the mechanism diagnostic predicts the economics:

    Spearman( E[ω/δ] , break-even cost ) = −0.943,  p = 0.0048
    Pearson                              = −0.845,  p = 0.034

Break-even cost changes sign exactly where E[ω/δ] crosses unity — interpolated break-even at
E[ω/δ] = 1.000 is +0.31 per leg. The overshoot deficit is not merely a descriptive statistic; the
unity crossing locates the profitability boundary.

This was computed after the primary result and is reported as an observation, not as a test.

## Post-hoc interpretation, explicitly untested

The adaptive rule pins δ*/R̂ at 0.32 by construction, so it lives permanently in one band. The two
winning fixed thresholds are large enough to reach the deepest band, 0.45–0.65, where ⟨ω⟩/δ ≈ 0.49,
on precisely the narrow-range sessions where the edge is largest. Targeting a fixed band may
therefore be the wrong control objective; the deepest reachable band may be the right one.

This hypothesis was generated by looking at the result. Under §7.2 the target 0.32 is **not** revised
and this run is **not** repeated. Any test of it requires a fresh pre-registration.
